\begin{abstract}
Standalone deep learning detectors for synthetic human faces routinely attain high in-distribution discrimination yet fail unpredictably under unseen generative architectures, external real-image domain shift, and lossy post-processing \cite{Bammey_2024,Khan_2024,Altamimi_2026,Guarnera_2024,Cheng_2024}. In operational digital forensics, returning an uncalibrated binary probability without provenance, uncertainty bounds, or abstention capability exposes investigators to silent false accusations and missed forgeries. This paper introduces the \textbf{AI Forensic Analyst}, an 18-module evidence-driven forensic architecture (M1--M18) that demotes the neural detector to one isolated, replaceable specialist (M4) inside a trust-bounded pipeline separating image-reading collectors from an image-blind four-way Decision Engine (M8: \textit{Real}, \textit{Fake}, \textit{Inconclusive}, \textit{Unknown}). First, we establish a strictly public-only, open-license five-dataset benchmark (ArtiFact faces, DiFF official test, 140k Real and Fake Faces, Synthbuster, and FairFace; over 250,000 processed images) with leakage-verified grouped and leave-one-generator-out splits and systematically stress-test a standardized ResNet-50 baseline. Although the baseline attains ROC-AUC 0.9261, PR-AUC 0.9348, balanced accuracy 0.8483, MCC 0.6978, and ECE 0.0525 on in-distribution ArtiFact test images ($n=7{,}505$, validation-frozen threshold $0.3951$), stress testing exposes 43.7\% FNR on StyleGAN3, balanced accuracy collapse toward 0.50 under fine-detail degradation (53 perturbations), 48.1\% unseen-generator cross-dataset FNR (50.2\% on DiFF), 30.4\%--82.6\% external real-face FPR, and a paired recall drop from 96.3\% to 13.7\% under controlled Stable Diffusion holdout. Second, we implement and verify the 18-module platform featuring reliability-weighted fusion (M7), validation-calibrated four-dimensional confidence (M9), programmatically evidence-verified reasoning (M11), structured eight-mode failure handling (M16), and a three-tier Django/React web application (M13--M14). Across 16 end-to-end verification scenarios, the platform passes all nine quality dimensions (135 verified reasoning sentences, zero ungrounded claims, 60.02 ms mean latency), explicitly overriding a 0.99 detector score into \textit{Inconclusive} ($\text{decision\_confidence}=0.3333$) when supporting evidence is unusable and degrading cleanly to \textit{Unknown} when specialists are unavailable. All code, registries, verification scripts, and datasets are publicly reproducible.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

High-fidelity facial synthesis has progressed rapidly from Generative Adversarial Networks (GANs) \cite{Guarnera_2020,Giudice_2021,Melnik_2024} to latent and denoising diffusion models \cite{Bammey_2024,Zeng_2025,Abbas_2026,Cheng_2024}, creating severe risks for identity verification, journalistic integrity, and judicial evidence authentication \cite{Heidari_2023,Edwards_2024,Malik_2022}. Despite strong benchmark accuracies reported on matched test splits, empirical evaluations consistently show that standalone classifiers overfit generator-specific spectral traces or dataset acquisition shortcuts \cite{Khan_2024,Altamimi_2026,Guarnera_2024}. When evaluated on unseen generative paradigms, demographically diverse external real images, or social-media compression pipelines, both false negative rates (missed forgeries) and false positive rates (authentic faces flagged as fake) spike sharply \cite{Bammey_2024,Sharma_2025,Altamimi_2026}.

This failure pattern reveals a structural flaw in how synthetic media detection is typically engineered. Most published systems conflate four distinct responsibilities inside a single black-box classifier: signal extraction, evidence reliability grading, uncertainty calibration, and final verdict issuance. A bare fake-class probability (for example, $0.99$) provides neither empirical reliability calibration nor a mechanism to abstain when input quality is degraded, corroborating signals are absent, or a specialist encounters an out-of-distribution domain \cite{Altamimi_2026,Guarnera_2024}.

To bridge the gap between model-centric detection research and auditable digital forensics, this study makes four concrete contributions:

\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{Controlled Cross-Paradigm Stress Benchmark:} We curate a strictly public-only, open-license five-dataset evaluation benchmark (ArtiFact faces, DiFF official test, 140k Real and Fake Faces, Synthbuster, and FairFace) under deterministic preprocessing and leakage-verified grouped and leave-one-generator-out (LOGO) splits, and quantify exact failure modes of a standardized ResNet-50 detector across calibration, 53 image perturbations, four external corpora, controlled Stable Diffusion holdout, four one-factor ablations, and paired statistical tests.
  \item \textbf{18-Module Trust-Bounded Forensic Architecture (M1--M18):} We design and implement the \textbf{AI Forensic Analyst}, separating image-reading intake, validation, nine independent non-detector collectors (M3), and an isolated deep learning specialist (M4) from an image-blind downstream core governed by an append-only evidence ledger (M5), non-destructive reliability validator (M6), pluggable fusion engine (M7), and four-way Decision Engine (M8: \textit{Real}, \textit{Fake}, \textit{Inconclusive}, \textit{Unknown}).
  \item \textbf{Validation-Calibrated Four-Dimensional Confidence and Anti-Hallucination Reasoning:} We formalize a four-dimensional calibration framework (M9) separating raw detector probability from empirical validation bin accuracy ($C_{\text{pred}}$), collector reliability ($C_{\text{ev}}$), execution completeness ($C_{\text{sys}}$), and capped decision confidence ($C_{\text{dec}}$). Downstream reasoning (M11) programmatically verifies every generated sentence against cited evidence ledger records, while explainability (M10) emits structured data-only outputs and explicitly records \texttt{no\_data} rather than fabricating visual maps.
  \item \textbf{End-to-End Platform Verification and Open Reproducibility:} We verify the complete system across 16 whole-platform forensic scenarios and nine quality dimensions, deliver an interactive three-tier Django/DRF and React web platform (M13--M14) validated by 85 automated unit/API/UI tests, live cross-tier HTTP integration, and six live User Acceptance Tests (UAT), and release all reproducible configurations, registries, and verification scripts under the MIT license.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Related Work}

We synthesize 28 peer-reviewed studies spanning legacy GAN forensics through modern diffusion detection \cite{Cho_2026,Taeb_2022,Bammey_2024,Ghani_2024,Guarnera_2020,Zeng_2025,Khan_2024,Pasquini_2023,Abbas_2026,Giudice_2021,Safwat_2024,Ul_Ghani_2024,Sharma_2025,Guarnera_2022,Xue_2023,Altamimi_2026,Preeti_2023,Guarnera_2024,Alkishri_2024,Nadimpalli_2024,Arshed_2024,Gragnaniello_2022,Cheng_2024,Heidari_2023,Melnik_2024,Edwards_2024,Dang_2023,Malik_2022} (full 16-method comparative matrix in Supplementary Material S1).

\textbf{Frequency-Domain and Convolutional Trace Detectors.} Early forensic pipelines exploited transposed-convolution upsampling footprints via Expectation-Maximization \cite{Guarnera_2020} and Discrete Cosine Transform (DCT) beta-statistics \cite{Giudice_2021}, achieving strong intra-GAN discrimination. Subsequent diffusion forensics revealed that iterative denoising alters high-frequency spectral signatures relative to GANs \cite{Bammey_2024}, motivating multi-level frequency and spatial hierarchies \cite{Guarnera_2024,Gragnaniello_2022}. However, frequency cues degrade sharply under JPEG recompression, resizing, and anti-forensic fingerprint removal \cite{Sharma_2025,Alkishri_2024}.

\textbf{Spatial Classifiers, Pretraining Paradigms, and Source Attribution.} Spatial CNNs (ResNet, Xception, EfficientNet) and Vision Transformers dominate large-scale benchmarks \cite{Taeb_2022,Safwat_2024,Guarnera_2022,Preeti_2023,Arshed_2024,Cheng_2024}. Systematic cross-architecture studies demonstrate that pretraining strategy (notably CLIP and self-supervised visual encoders) strongly influences cross-generator transfer \cite{Khan_2024}, while hierarchical classifiers attempt generator-family attribution \cite{Guarnera_2024,Arshed_2024}. Nevertheless, recent multi-paradigm audits confirm that classifiers trained on GAN corpora suffer severe accuracy drops on diffusion faces and exhibit demographic and dataset-origin bias shortcuts \cite{Altamimi_2026,Cheng_2024}.

\textbf{Physiological Biometrics, Reconstruction Error, and Proactive Defenses.} Alternative paradigms target facial landmark geometry and GAN-inversion biometrics \cite{Pasquini_2023,Xue_2023}, diffusion reconstruction error \cite{Zeng_2025}, synthetic inpainting generation \cite{Cho_2026}, and proactive watermarking or blockchain provenance \cite{Ghani_2024,Ul_Ghani_2024,Nadimpalli_2024}. Biometric cues lose discriminative margin on high-fidelity StyleGAN3 and diffusion synthesis \cite{Melnik_2024}, reconstruction error imposes heavy multi-step inference cost \cite{Zeng_2025}, and proactive schemes require control over image generation \cite{Nadimpalli_2024}. Existing surveys \cite{Heidari_2023,Melnik_2024,Edwards_2024,Dang_2023,Malik_2022} comprehensively catalog neural architectures yet rarely address system-level forensic decision governance.

\textbf{Synthesized Gaps.} Four interlocking gaps motivate the AI Forensic Analyst: (1) cross-generator generalization collapse between GAN and diffusion families, (2) real-source shortcut learning causing high false positive rates on out-of-distribution authentic faces, (3) insufficient uncertainty calibration and explainability transparency \cite{Altamimi_2026}, and (4) absence of modular, auditable evidence-fusion architectures capable of safe abstention.

All images pass through a deterministic seven-stage preprocessing pipeline (fixed seed 42): integrity verification, pre-modification metadata capture, EXIF stripping, minimum resolution filtering ($\ge 64$\,px), RGB PNG conversion, and $224\times 224$ resizing with zero denoising or sharpening so high-frequency forensic traces remain intact. On ArtiFact, we generate two leakage-verified splits: a grouped random split ($34{,}993$ train / $7{,}502$ validation / $7{,}505$ test) and a controlled leave-one-generator-out (LOGO) split holding out all $1{,}923$ Stable Diffusion images exclusively for testing. Automated hash and identifier checks confirm zero cross-split overlap (exploratory audits in Supplementary Material S2).

\subsection{Trust-Bounded 18-Module Forensic Pipeline}

\begin{figure*}[t]
  \centering
  \includegraphics[width=\textwidth]{figures/FIG0027_v1.pdf}
  \caption{Eighteen-module AI Forensic Analyst architecture separating Trust Boundary A (image-reading intake, validation, nine independent signal collectors, and isolated deep learning specialist) from Trust Boundary B (image-blind append-only evidence ledger, reliability validator, fusion engine, four-dimensional confidence estimator, sole four-way Decision Engine, data-only explainability, grounded reasoning, and report generator) and delivery/governance modules (M13--M18).}
  \label{fig:architecture}
\end{figure*}

Figure~\ref{fig:architecture} illustrates the architectural boundary enforcing forensic accountability across all 18 modules (M1--M18; complete interface summary in Supplementary Material S4):

\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Trust Boundary A (Image-Reading Layer, Zero Decision Authority):} Input Intake (M1) enforces format and 20\,MB upload limits while computing SHA-256 hashes and rejecting unsafe files without persistence. Image Validation (M2) verifies decodability, RGB channels, resolution, noise, and single-face availability; violations trigger immediate structured stops (\texttt{unsupported\_format}, \texttt{corrupted\_image}, \texttt{no\_face}, \texttt{multiple\_faces}) before collectors run, omitting verdicts while logging a failed case record (M15). Passing inputs reach nine strictly decoupled non-detector collectors (M3: metadata, image statistics, face detection, face quality, FFT frequency domain, compression, color distribution, texture, and explainability input; zero inter-collector coupling) and the isolated Deep Learning Specialist (M4), which emits raw $P(\text{fake})$, latency, and model provenance without applying any decision threshold or verdict word.
  \item \textbf{Trust Boundary B (Image-Blind Evidence and Decision Layer):} The Evidence Ledger (M5) assigns sequential immutable identifiers (\texttt{EVxxxxxx}) in an append-only ledger. The Evidence Validator (M6) appends separate validation records grading each item as \texttt{high}, \texttt{medium}, \texttt{low}, or \texttt{unusable} and cross-citing conflicts without mutating source rows or substituting defaults for \texttt{null} confidences. Downstream modules read strictly validated evidence IDs and never access raw images or model weights.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Reliability-Weighted Fusion, 4D Calibrated Confidence, and 4-Way Decisioning}

\begin{figure*}[t]
  \centering
  \includegraphics[width=\textwidth]{figures/FIG0028_v1.pdf}
  \caption{Mathematical flow from the validated evidence ledger through reliability-weighted rule-based fusion (M7), validation-calibrated four-dimensional confidence (M9), and image-blind four-way verdict issuance (M8). Safety caps override a raw $0.99$ detector score into \textit{Inconclusive} ($C_{\text{dec}}=0.3333$) whenever supporting evidence is unusable.}
  \label{fig:fusion}
\end{figure*}

As illustrated in Figure~\ref{fig:fusion}, the mathematical decision core operates in three transparent stages:

\textbf{1. Reliability-Weighted Evidence Fusion (M7).} Under a pluggable \texttt{FusionStrategy} interface (active strategy: interpretable \texttt{rule\_based} v0.1.0; \texttt{weighted} and \texttt{bayesian} strategies reserved; unknown IDs fail loudly via M16), each validated item receives weight $w_i \in \{1.0, 0.6, 0.3, 0.0\}$ for \texttt{high}, \texttt{medium}, \texttt{low}, and \texttt{unusable} respectively. Only calibrated directional specialists vote on class polarity: for M4 with fake probability $p$ and validation-frozen threshold $t = 0.3951$, $p \ge t$ contributes $+w_i$ (\texttt{positive\_class}) and $p < t$ contributes $-w_i$ (\texttt{negative\_class}). The nine contextual M3 collectors are cited as corroborating context without fabricating directional votes. When opposing directional evidence yields normalized $|\text{balance}| < 0.5$, the fused lean is \texttt{contested}; when no usable directional specialist exists, the lean is \texttt{no\_directional\_evidence}. The emitted fused conclusion contains zero verdict words and is itself logged (M5) and graded (M6).

\textbf{2. Validation-Calibrated Four-Dimensional Confidence (M9).} To break the equivalence between raw softmax probability and forensic certainty \cite{Altamimi_2026}, M9 computes four distinct metrics:
\begin{equation}
  C_{\text{pred}} = w_{\text{det}} \cdot A_{\text{val}}(r), \quad
  r = \begin{cases} p & \text{if } p \ge t, \\ 1 - p & \text{if } p < t, \end{cases}
\end{equation}
where $A_{\text{val}}(r)$ is the empirical bin accuracy queried from validation-split reliability bins (\texttt{null} if no usable detector item exists). For example, baseline output $p = 0.99$ maps to $C_{\text{pred}} = 0.971745$ rather than $0.99$. Next, evidence reliability confidence is the mean validator weight across all collector items (\texttt{unusable} $= 0.0$), halved whenever a conflict flag is active:
\begin{equation}
  C_{\text{ev}} = \left(\frac{1}{N} \sum_{i=1}^{N} w_i\right) \cdot \begin{cases} 0.5 & \text{if conflict}, \\ 1.0 & \text{otherwise}. \end{cases}
\end{equation}
System execution completeness measures pipeline health:
\begin{equation}
  C_{\text{sys}} = \frac{N_{\text{usable}}}{N_{\text{attempted}} + N_{\text{refused}} + N_{\text{errors}}}.
\end{equation}
Finally, overall decision confidence combines the three dimensions with explicit conservative caps and discounts:
\begin{equation}
  C_{\text{base}} = 0.5\,C_{\text{pred}} + 0.3\,C_{\text{ev}} + 0.2\,C_{\text{sys}}.
\end{equation}
If $C_{\text{pred}}$ is \texttt{null}, then $C_{\text{dec}}$ remains \texttt{null}. Otherwise, whenever $C_{\text{ev}} \le 0.50$, $C_{\text{sys}} \le 0.50$, or zero usable non-detector collectors exist, $C_{\text{dec}}$ is capped at $\min(C_{\text{ev}}, C_{\text{sys}})$; single-source leans receive a $\times 0.85$ discount and contested leans receive a $\times 0.50$ discount.

\textbf{3. Image-Blind Four-Way Decision Engine (M8).} Reading solely the validated fused conclusion and confidence ledger IDs, M8 emits: \textit{Unknown} if \texttt{no\_directional\_evidence} (or $C_{\text{dec}}$ is \texttt{null}); \textit{Inconclusive} if the lean is \texttt{contested}, a conflict flag is active, or $C_{\text{dec}} < 0.50$; \textit{Fake} if \texttt{positive\_class}, no conflict, and $C_{\text{dec}} \ge 0.50$; and \textit{Real} if \texttt{negative\_class}, no conflict, and $C_{\text{dec}} \ge 0.50$.

\subsection{Grounded Reasoning, Data-Only Explainability, and Web Delivery}

Explainability (M10) emits structured JSON across Grad-CAM, attention maps, saliency, and integrated gradients (with SHAP, LIME, and counterfactuals reserved), explicitly recording \texttt{no\_data} (\texttt{data: null}) for unrecorded visual maps and refusing fabricated overlays or verdict strings. Grounded Reasoning (M11) constructs \texttt{reasoning.json} strictly from validated ledger records and executes programmatic verification on every sentence to confirm that every cited evidence ID and numeric value matches the ledger; any ungrounded claim raises a structured M16 error. The Report Generator (M12) outputs reproducible JSON, Markdown, and HTML reports stamped with byte-identical system, model, dataset, experiment, and Git commit provenance. Full-stack web delivery (Supplementary Figure S4) exposes 10 canonical endpoints across a seven-app Django/DRF backend (M13, zero duplicated forensic logic, thread-safe lock serialization) consumed by a 14-page, 24-component React/Vite/Tailwind UI (M14) rendering nine live pipeline stages, four-way verdicts, all four calibrated confidence gauges, cited evidence IDs, and explicit limitations.

\section{Experimental Setup}

All neural training ran on NVIDIA T4 GPUs under a locked environment (Python 3.12, PyTorch 2.10.0+cu128, FastAI 2.8.7) governed by immutable YAML configurations (47 schema validation tests) and SHA-256 atomic checkpoints (Supplementary Figure S5). Six controlled experiments were executed:

\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Baseline ResNet-50:} ImageNet-1k pretrained ResNet-50 trained on the ArtiFact grouped random split ($224\times 224$, Adam $\text{wd}=0.01$, FP16 mixed precision, 1 frozen + 10 unfrozen epochs, train-only geometric/photometric augmentation, early-stopping patience 3). Operating threshold $t = 0.3951$ (exact $0.395057$) maximizing balanced accuracy was frozen strictly on the validation split.
  \item \textbf{Unseen-Generator Holdout (LOGO):} Identical baseline hyperparameters trained on the ArtiFact LOGO split excluding all Stable Diffusion images, evaluated on all $1{,}923$ held-out Stable Diffusion images and paired directly against the Baseline ResNet-50 on the identical 299 Stable Diffusion test images shared by both splits.
  \item \textbf{Controlled One-Factor Ablations:} Four variants each mutating exactly one baseline hyperparameter on the identical ArtiFact split: (1) random seed $42 \rightarrow 43$ (repeatability noise floor), (2) train-only augmentation disabled, (3) ImageNet-1k pretraining disabled under the fixed 11-epoch budget, and (4) input spatial resolution reduced ($224 \rightarrow 160$\,px).
  \item \textbf{Stress Testing and Paired Statistics:} Baseline evaluation covers calibration (ECE, MCE, Brier), operating points (screening $0.089$, frozen $0.3951$, flag-for-review $0.629$), subgroup error breakdowns, 53 synthetic perturbations across all $7{,}505$ test images, and four external benchmarks (DiFF, 140k Real and Fake Faces, Synthbuster, FairFace). Paired statistical tests match all $7{,}505$ test predictions via 10,000 percentile bootstrap resamples (seed 49) on ROC-AUC (95\% CI for the seed replicate; Bonferroni-adjusted 98.3\% CIs across the three factor ablations) and exact two-sided McNemar tests. Because each ablation ran for one seed and Wilcoxon signed-rank across seeds requires at least six pairs ($n=2<6$), all comparisons report saved-run differences with no cross-seed superiority claim.
  \item \textbf{Whole-Platform and Full-Stack Verification:} The 18-module system was verified across 16 end-to-end forensic scenarios spanning nine quality dimensions, 85 automated unit/API/UI tests, live cross-tier HTTP integration, and six live UAT scenarios.
\end{itemize}

\section{Results}

\subsection{In-Distribution Baseline Performance, Calibration, and Subgroup Failures}

On the ArtiFact test split ($n=7{,}505$: $3{,}753$ fake, $3{,}752$ real), the Baseline ResNet-50 attains ROC-AUC $0.9261$, PR-AUC $0.9348$, balanced accuracy $0.8483$, MCC $0.6978$, FNR $0.1829$ (686 missed fakes), FPR $0.1205$ (452 false alarms), Brier score $0.1131$, ECE $0.0525$, and MCE $0.1544$ (additional curves in Supplementary Material S3).

\subsection{Perturbation Robustness Across 53 Image Distortions}

\begin{figure*}[t]
  \centering
  \includegraphics[width=\textwidth]{figures/FIG0016_v1.pdf}
  \caption{Balanced accuracy and ROC-AUC under 53 synthetic perturbations ($n=7{,}505$ test images per condition). Fine-detail degradations (low-quality upload, downscaling, Gaussian blur, strong noise, and JPEG quality 10) collapse balanced accuracy toward 0.50 by driving FPR toward 1.0, whereas moderate photometric changes cost under 0.10.}
  \label{fig:robustness}
\end{figure*}

Across all 53 perturbations (Figure~\ref{fig:robustness}), robustness splits into two distinct regimes: (1) \textbf{catastrophic fine-detail collapse}, where low-quality uploads, downscaling, Gaussian blur, strong noise, and JPEG quality 10 push balanced accuracy to $\approx 0.50$ because the baseline flags nearly every degraded real image as fake ($\text{FPR}\rightarrow 1.0$), and (2) \textbf{moderate photometric resilience}, where brightness, contrast, color jitter, and small rotations reduce balanced accuracy by $<0.10$. Separating ROC-AUC from thresholded balanced accuracy shows that mild compression/resizing introduces threshold shift alongside genuine ranking erosion under heavy blur and noise.

Cross-dataset testing (Figure~\ref{fig:cross_dataset}) confirms both halves of detector fragility: across 17 unseen generators, FNR rises from $18.3\%$ in-distribution to $48.1\%$ ($50.2\%$ on DiFF diffusion faces, reaching $81.5\%$ on FreeDoM\_I), whereas familiar StyleGAN-family fakes in 140k Real and Fake Faces incur only $9.0\%$ FNR. Simultaneously, external authentic images trigger extreme false alarms: $30.4\%$ FPR on 140k FFHQ, $76.7\%$ FPR on Synthbuster RAISE scenes (overall Synthbuster ROC-AUC $0.5172$, near chance), and $82.6\%$ FPR on FairFace across all seven demographic groups. Controlled Stable Diffusion holdout (Figure~\ref{fig:unseen_logo}) isolates pure unseen-generator failure without dataset shift: while the LOGO model retains validation ROC-AUC $0.937$ on its 12 seen generators, held-out Stable Diffusion recall drops to $15.2\%$ ($293/1{,}923$), falling from $96.3\%$ to $13.7\%$ on the exact same 299 test images \cite{Khan_2024,Altamimi_2026,Cheng_2024}. Consequently, the standalone detector is explicitly classified as unsuitable for autonomous deployment.

\subsection{Whole-Platform Forensic Analyst and Full-Stack Verification}

In whole-platform evaluation (16 end-to-end scenarios in Supplementary Material S4), the AI Forensic Analyst passes \textbf{16/16 scenarios} and \textbf{9/9 quality dimensions} with \textbf{135 programmatically verified reasoning sentences}, \textbf{zero ungrounded leaks}, \textbf{zero silent failures}, and \textbf{60.02\,ms mean latency}. Key verified safety behaviors include:

\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{High-Confidence Safety Override:} When M4 emits $P(\text{fake})=0.99$ ($C_{\text{pred}}=0.9717$) while supporting collectors are unusable ($C_{\text{ev}}=0.3333$), the conservative cap overrides the raw neural score to $C_{\text{dec}}=0.3333$ and M8 issues \textit{Inconclusive} instead of a blind \textit{Fake} verdict.
  \item \textbf{Graceful Specialist Degradation:} Missing model weights or specialist timeouts degrade cleanly to \textit{Unknown} with \texttt{null} prediction and decision confidences while preserving all nine contextual collectors.
  \item \textbf{Anti-Hallucination Reasoning Enforcement:} Deliberately injected ungrounded claims or invalid evidence references are deterministically rejected by M11 via M16.
  \item \textbf{Full-Stack Web Verification:} All 85 automated unit/API/frontend tests (55 forensic core, 18 Django/DRF backend, 12 React frontend), production Vite build, live cross-tier async HTTP integration, and all six live UAT scenarios pass with zero leaked stack traces.
\end{itemize}

\section{Discussion and Limitations}

Our empirical findings underscore a foundational principle for digital forensics: even a well-calibrated, leakage-free neural detector ($\text{ROC-AUC } 0.9261$, $\text{ECE } 0.0525$) exhibits catastrophic failures once exposed to unseen diffusion generators ($13.7\%$ paired recall), external authentic face distributions ($82.6\%$ FairFace FPR), or heavy post-processing ($\approx 0.50$ balanced accuracy). Deploying such a classifier as an autonomous binary arbiter is scientifically indefensible. Structurally isolating perception (M3--M4) from immutable evidence logging (M5--M6), validation-calibrated four-dimensional confidence (M9), and an image-blind four-way Decision Engine (M8) transforms silent model failure into explicit \textit{Inconclusive} or \textit{Unknown} abstentions backed by auditable provenance.

Six Version 1 scope boundaries are disclosed transparently: (1) only one CNN backbone family (ResNet-50) was trained across six runs, leaving Xception, EfficientNet-B4, and self-supervised ViT-B/16 \cite{Khan_2024} for Version 2; (2) factor ablations ran for one seed each (two baseline seeds total, so Wilcoxon across seeds is deferred and no cross-seed superiority is claimed), and the no-pretraining run reflects a fixed 11-epoch single-factor comparison rather than a separately tuned schedule; (3) the nine non-detector collectors currently provide reliability-graded context under interpretable rule-based fusion, whereas learned weighted or Bayesian fusion awaits curated labeled multi-collector forensic corpora; (4) LOGO evaluation held out Stable Diffusion only (fake-only recall), and perturbations were applied post-preprocessing to $224$\,px crops; (5) runtime Grad-CAM/SHAP/LIME tensor producers remain reserved for Version 2 (M10 accurately records \texttt{no\_data} when visual maps are absent); and (6) Version 1 is scoped to static single-face images and local execution, with multimodal video/audio/text extensions via M18, production TLS/authentication hardening, and practitioner human-factors trials planned for Version 2.

\section{Conclusion}

Trustworthy synthetic face forensics requires shifting from standalone binary classification to auditable, uncertainty-aware forensic systems. Across five public datasets, six controlled GPU experiments, and multi-axis stress testing, we quantified the exact unseen-generator ($13.7\%$ paired Stable Diffusion recall), external authentic-face ($82.6\%$ FairFace FPR), and fine-detail perturbation failures of a standardized ResNet-50 baseline. We then designed, implemented, and verified the 18-module AI Forensic Analyst (M1--M18), demonstrating across 16 end-to-end scenarios (135 verified reasoning sentences, zero ungrounded leaks, 60.02\,ms mean latency) and full-stack web verification (85 automated tests plus six live UAT scenarios) how trust-boundary isolation, reliability-weighted fusion, validation-calibrated four-dimensional confidence, and four-way abstention prevent overconfident neural probabilities from becoming silent forensic errors.
